# Market Research — Boredom Breaker

**Status:** Not yet validated  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-15

## Research objective
Determine whether a specific group of people has a frequent, meaningful problem finding worthwhile activities during free time, and identify what current solutions fail to do well.

## Current hypotheses (not facts)
- People sometimes have free time but experience decision fatigue about how to use it.
- Existing alternatives include search engines, social platforms, saved lists, event/activity apps and recommendation tools.
- A useful opportunity may exist around matching ideas to time, mood, energy, context or constraints.

## Competitor review template
For each alternative, record:
- Target user and core promise.
- Main discovery and recommendation flow.
- What users praise.
- Repeated complaints, friction and missing capabilities.
- Pricing and monetisation model (if public).
- Evidence links and date checked.
- Specific gap Boredom Breaker could address.

## Questions to answer
1. How often does the problem occur, and in what situations?
2. What do people do today instead?
3. What makes them abandon an activity suggestion?
4. Which constraints matter most: time, cost, location, materials, mood, energy, company or safety?
5. What would make someone return weekly?
6. Is the unmet need strong enough to support a business?

## Research quality rules
- Prefer direct user reviews, discussion threads, interviews and observed behaviour.
- Separate direct evidence from interpretation.
- Record dates and links.
- Look for repeated patterns, not isolated complaints.
- Do not claim product-market fit or willingness to pay without evidence.
