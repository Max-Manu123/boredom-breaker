# Boredom Breaker — Product Blueprint

**Status:** Working draft — product assumptions require validation  
**Version:** Foundation 0.1  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-15

## 1. Product summary
Boredom Breaker helps people turn unstructured free time into an activity they can actually start. It offers simple ideas organised by mood/category, time required and effort, with enough guidance to get going.

## 2. User and problem hypothesis
- **Initial audience hypothesis:** people who have free time but struggle to decide what to do, especially when they want something more intentional than endless scrolling.
- **Problem hypothesis:** choosing an activity can take more effort than starting one; generic lists often lack useful context such as time, materials, steps or energy required.
- **Primary job to be done:** “When I have free time and don't know what to do, help me find an enjoyable, realistic activity I can start now.”

These are hypotheses, not validated market facts. Research must establish which audience experiences this often enough to return and whether existing alternatives leave a meaningful gap.

## 3. Current product promise
Find a small activity that fits your mood and available time, then get clear steps to begin.

## 4. Existing MVP scope (from current repository)
- 24 curated activities in Games, Create, Move, Explore, Learn and Relax.
- Surprise Me activity suggestion.
- Activity browsing, text search and duration filter.
- Activity details with materials, steps and a Bori tip.
- Favorites stored in browser localStorage.
- English/Portuguese interface toggle.
- Responsive desktop/mobile layouts.

## 5. Explicitly out of scope until validated
- User accounts, cloud sync and social profiles.
- AI-generated activity recommendations.
- Payments/subscriptions.
- Social feeds, chat and public user-generated content.
- Complex streaks, challenges or habit tracking.

## 6. Success questions
1. Can a new user find a suitable activity quickly?
2. Do users start or complete suggested activities?
3. Do they return when they have free time again?
4. Which categories, durations and recommendation paths are most useful?
5. Is there a repeated unmet need that justifies a paid tier?

## 7. Main risks
- The app may be a novelty rather than a recurring need.
- A catalogue of generic ideas may be easy to copy and hard to monetise.
- Static content may become repetitive.
- “Surprise Me” may not feel relevant without mood/energy context.
- A bilingual toggle may translate interface copy but not all activity content.

## 8. Decisions still open
- Narrowest audience with the strongest recurring problem.
- Most valuable differentiation versus search engines, social media, activity lists and competitor apps.
- Whether the next version should focus on better discovery, personalised fit, fresh content or another validated need.
- Monetisation only after repeat value is demonstrated.
